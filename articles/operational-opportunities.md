# Operational Opportunities and Next Steps

This chapter collects the operating questions raised by the earlier
pages. Each action below is a hypothesis to investigate. The chapter has
no forecast, no price elasticity, and no revenue estimate. The weather
and elasticity vignette is a separate synthetic exercise. It does not
change the figures in this chapter.

Utilization is golfers divided by the city’s daylight estimate. The city
sets that estimate from sunrise to sunset. Golfers per day is the count
of people who played. A day above one hundred percent ran past the
estimate. Tee-sheet occupancy would require a tee time, and this extract
has daily counts.

The rates use the 1,708 days with at least one golfer, from 2021-07-01
through 2026-04-30. A period rate is the sum of golfers divided by the
sum of the daylight estimates, the same ratio as the capacity tables.
October closures are omitted with every other day that has no play.

## Opportunities to investigate

Each block is a hypothesis to investigate.

### Summer daylight share

**Evidence.** July is 64% of the daylight estimate (252 golfers per open
day). June is 68% (275). August is 68% (255). December is 85% (231
golfers).

**Hypothesis.** Summer fills a smaller share of a longer daylight
estimate. Headcount on those open days stays near the winter months.

**Potential investigation.** Look at summer afternoons, and at the mix
of 9-hole and 18-hole play, before treating the whole day as unused.

**Additional data needed.** Tee times, hole counts, and prices paid by
time of day.

### February, March, and April

**Evidence.** February is 90%, with 28 of 141 open days above the
estimate. March is 91%, with 20 of 155. April has 314 golfers per open
day, the most, at 86%. Realized green fees are about \$46 to \$48 per
golfer in February and March, and about \$16 to \$19 in June through
August. Daily green fees correlate with golfers at 0.30 in 2024 and 0.20
in 2025.

**Hypothesis.** The winter receipt per golfer is already higher. This
file cannot show whether a higher posted rate would hold the headcount.

**Potential investigation.** Compare the rate card with the rate codes
used in February, March, and April before any peak-rate test.

**Additional data needed.** Prices paid, rate code, 9-hole and 18-hole
mix, and any count of golfers who could not get a time.

### Tuesday and Wednesday

**Evidence.** Wednesday is 74% and Tuesday is 75%, against Sunday at
84%. That weekday gap is 10 percentage points. July to March is 27
percentage points. February Wednesday is 78%, and March Wednesday is
88%.

**Hypothesis.** The soft weekday is concentrated in summer. One Tuesday
rate would also cover months that are already in the high band.

**Potential investigation.** Review Tuesday and Wednesday one month at a
time before writing a standing weekday rate.

**Additional data needed.** Tee times by hour, booking lead time, and
the customer segment on those days.

### Cart receipts in 2025

**Evidence.** In 2022, 2023, and 2024 the receipt per recorded cart is
\$16.00. Carts per golfer run from 0.78 to 0.80, and cart dollars per
golfer run from \$12.50 to \$12.77. In 2025 those figures are \$10.57,
0.42, and \$4.41. The city catalog says the cart count is derived from
cart revenue. From January through April 2026, cart dollars per golfer
are \$4.09. Total dollars per golfer move from \$99.41 in 2024 to
\$101.99 in 2025.

**Hypothesis.** The cart product, the cart price, or the derivation of
the cart count changed in 2025. Cart revenue correlates with golfers at
0.79 to 0.83 in 2022 through 2024, and at 0.40 in 2025.

**Potential investigation.** Reconcile 2025 cart tenders and the
derivation rule before treating the drop as lower demand for carts.

**Additional data needed.** The posted cart price, whether the cart is
included in the green fee, and a cart count taken independently of
revenue.

### Range receipts

**Evidence.** Range dollars per golfer are \$11.63 in 2022 and \$27.13
in 2025. The correlation with daily golfers is 0.20 in 2022 and 0.09 in
2025.

**Hypothesis.** Range receipts rose while moving less closely with the
golfer count. Someone who only used the range is not in that count.

**Potential investigation.** Ask what changed in the range price, the
range volume, or who can buy range balls.

**Additional data needed.** Range transactions separated from rounds.

## What Additional Data Would Unlock

Reservation-level tee times would place open summer daylight. Booking
lead times would show if February and March fill early. Prices paid,
with the rate code, would separate winter and summer green fees.
Cancellations, customer segments (resident, junior, 9-hole, and
18-hole), and weather would cover a full sheet, who plays, and how much
of the gap follows the heat.
